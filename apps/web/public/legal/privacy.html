Privacy Policy

Last updated 25 October 2026

1. Who we are

Merkur is a personal project of Dmytro Pletenskyi, a private individual who controls your personal data. Contact: dmitriy.pletenskoy@gmail.com. Postal address: see our Contact / Impressum page. No data protection officer is required or appointed.

2. What we are built not to see

Merkur is designed so that your password (sign-in uses OPAQUE, so it is not sent to us), the contents of your terminal (encrypted end to end between your browser and your machine) and your private keys stay with you. Our relay passes encrypted traffic along; it can see connection details such as IP addresses, which account and machine a connection belongs to, and the size and timing of that traffic.

Before you first open a terminal, the app asks whether you want to share performance reports, with neither answer preselected. Your browser and linked machines send reports if you say yes. They are designed to leave out what you type and what your terminal shows. You can change your choice at any time under Settings, Account; when you turn them off, your browser and linked machines stop sending them and our server refuses them.

4. Cookies

We use only a strictly necessary cookie to keep you signed in. The app stores your settings and keys in your browser because it needs them to work. We do not use cookies or browser storage for tracking or advertising.

5. Where your data is stored

Our providers are Hetzner and Axiom (Germany), Resend (Ireland), and Cloudflare and GitHub (United States). Hetzner runs our servers in Germany: the app, its database, the relay and the website. Cloudflare carries the website's traffic and protects it from attacks; it carries neither the app nor your terminal sessions. Cloudflare also stores our backups in its EU data region; they are encrypted on our servers before they leave them. Website analytics run on our own installation at Hetzner and are not shared with an analytics company. Providers that process data on our behalf do so under data processing terms; GitHub receives download requests from the daemon under its own privacy terms. If you turn on notifications, they are delivered through your browser vendor's push service (for example Apple, Google or Mozilla), and their content is encrypted. Transfers outside the EEA, including to the US, and access from outside the EEA are protected by the EU Standard Contractual Clauses or the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, which are published by the European Commission and the US Department of Commerce.

6. Sharing

We do not sell your data, use it for advertising or send marketing emails. We disclose data to authorities only when the law requires it.

7. How long we keep data

  • Account data and linked machines: while your account exists; erased shortly after you delete it, normally within 7 days.
  • Inactive accounts with email sign-in: deleted after 12 months without sign-in and 30 days' email notice. Signing in cancels deletion. Username-only accounts are not deleted for inactivity because they have no verified email address.
  • Browser sessions: up to 30 days, or until you sign out or revoke them.
  • Verification codes and sign-in attempt counters: briefly, from minutes to about an hour.
  • Push endpoint: until you turn off notifications or delete your account.
  • Logs, relay connection data and performance reports: up to about 30 days.
  • Website analytics: visits cannot be linked to you after the day they occur; aggregated statistics are kept only while useful for understanding website use.
  • Waitlist email: until we send that one email, you withdraw consent, or 12 months pass.
  • Abuse reports: 12 months; suspended accounts, including their emails: deleted 24 months after suspension.
  • Backups: the database and website analytics are backed up regularly, encrypted, and kept for about five weeks, so deleted data can remain in a backup for that long. If we restore a backup, we apply again the deletions made since it was taken.
  • If the service shuts down: personal data is deleted within a reasonable time, normally 30 days, unless the law requires otherwise.

These periods are enforced by regular automated cleanup, so deletion can happen shortly after a period ends rather than at its exact end.

We keep data longer only where the law requires it or to deal with a security incident or legal claim.

8. Your rights

You may access, correct, delete, restrict or export your data, and withdraw consent at any time. Delete your account under Settings, Account; for anything else, email us. We reply within the time the law sets, normally one month, free of charge. You may complain to a data protection authority in the EU country where you live, work or where the issue occurred, or in Ukraine to the Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights.

9. Your right to object

You may object at any time to processing based on our legitimate interests. We will stop unless we have compelling grounds or need the data for legal claims.

10. Security

We protect data with measures such as end-to-end encryption, OPAQUE sign-in and signed releases (details). No system is fully secure. If a breach affects your data, we will notify the authorities and you where the law requires.

11. Children

The service is not for anyone under 18. If we learn we hold a child's data, we delete it.

12. Transfer of the service

If the service passes to a new operator, we will tell you who it is in advance, and you may delete your account first. The new operator will become responsible for your data.

13. Changes

We will post changes here with a new date and notify you of material changes in advance. Questions: dmitriy.pletenskoy@gmail.com.
